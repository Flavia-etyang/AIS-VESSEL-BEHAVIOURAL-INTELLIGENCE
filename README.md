**IMO:** 9251951

### Intelligence Question

> How significant is the documented AIS interruption when assessed alongside the vessel's identity, STS activity and independent regulatory findings?

---

## 01 Vessel Identification

**Vessel:** MS ENOLA  
**IMO:** 9251951  
**Flag:** Djibouti  
**Vessel Type:** Crude oil tanker

>  **The U.S. Department of the Treasury identified MS ENOLA (IMO 9251951) as a Djibouti-flagged crude oil tanker. Treasury identified Journey Investment Company as the registered owner and Rose Shipping Limited as manager/operator.**
> 
---

## 02 Documented Regulatory Finding

According to the U.S. Department of the Treasury's 19 December 2024 action, MS ENOLA had been involved in the transfer of Iranian oil and had recently received Iranian oil through a ship-to-ship transfer from DINO I (IMO 9569671).

Treasury also stated that MS ENOLA used AIS shutdowns as part of deceptive practices.

---

## 03 AIS Behaviour

### Documented indicator

Treasury reported that MS ENOLA had used AIS shutdowns as part of deceptive practices.

### Analytical significance

An AIS transmission interruption can have multiple explanations and should not, in isolation, be treated as proof of intentional concealment.

Its significance increases when considered alongside independent evidence such as:

- documented STS activity
- vessel identity
- sanctions or regulatory findings
- cargo / trade context
- repeated or strategically timed transmission gaps

---

## 04 STS Correlation

Treasury stated that MS ENOLA had recently received Iranian oil through an STS transfer from DINO I (IMO 9569671).

The STS finding provides an independent behavioural context for the reported AIS interruptions.

### Correlation

| Evidence Layer | Finding | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Vessel identity | MS ENOLA, IMO 9251951 | Establishes subject vessel |
| AIS behaviour | AIS shutdowns reported by Treasury | Behavioural indicator |
| STS activity | Iranian oil received via STS from DINO I | Independent contextual evidence |
| Regulatory action | U.S. Treasury designation | External corroboration |
| Ownership | Journey Investment Company | Entity linkage |

---

## 06 Contradictions & Evidence Gaps

The available source material documents the vessel's identity, ownership/management relationships, STS activity and reported AIS shutdowns.

However, this case does not independently reproduce the underlying AIS track or transmission-gap chronology.

Therefore, the portfolio does not independently establish:

- the exact duration of each AIS interruption
- the precise geographic location of each interruption
- whether each interruption was manually initiated
- the complete sequence of AIS behaviour surrounding the STS event

These remain dependent on the underlying source material.

---

## 07 Assessment

> The documented AIS interruptions are significant because they are reported in the context of independently documented STS activity involving Iranian oil.
> 
> The U.S. Department of the Treasury specifically characterized the AIS shutdowns as part of deceptive practices involving MS ENOLA.
> 
> From an analytical perspective, AIS loss alone would not establish deceptive behaviour. In this case, its significance is increased by the documented vessel identity, STS activity and regulatory finding.

---

## 08 Confidence

**Confidence: HIGH**

Confidence is HIGH for the documented vessel identity and regulatory findings because these are directly supported by the cited U.S. Treasury action.

Confidence is lower for any independent reconstruction of the underlying AIS timeline because the raw AIS track data is not reproduced in this case study.


## Analyst Note

> AIS interruption should be treated as an indicator rather than standalone proof of deceptive behaviour. Its analytical significance increases when corroborated with independent evidence such as STS activity, vessel identity, ownership relationships, sanctions findings and regulatory actions.

# AIS & VESSEL BEHAVIOURAL INTELLIGENCE

**Track · Observe · Detect · Correlate · Assess**

Transforming vessel movement data into structured intelligence through trajectory analysis, anomaly detection and evidence-based assessment.

</div>

---

## Intelligence Question

### What does a vessel's movement and transmitted AIS data reveal about its observed behaviour?

AIS can provide information including a vessel's identity, position, speed, course, heading and navigational status.

However, AIS data alone does not explain **why** a vessel behaved in a particular way.

The objective of this analysis is to establish:

- Where the vessel was observed
- How the vessel moved
- What behaviour or anomaly was identified
- Whether the behaviour is consistent with its operational context
- What other information supports or challenges the observation
- What conclusion can be justified by the available evidence

> **Core principle:** An AIS anomaly is an intelligence lead — not an automatic conclusion.

---

# 01 — Observation

The analysis begins with the available AIS record.

The first question is:

> **What does the transmitted data directly show?**

Observable information may include:

- Vessel position
- Time and date
- Speed over ground
- Course over ground
- Heading
- Navigational status
- Rate of movement
- Track direction
- Position-report continuity
- Reported destination and ETA, where available

The vessel track is first treated as an observation record.

```text
POSITION
    +
TIME
    +
SPEED
    +
COURSE
    ↓
OBSERVED MOVEMENT
```

Examples of observable events may include:

- Change in speed
- Change in course
- Prolonged stationary position
- Drifting
- Route deviation
- At-sea encounter
- AIS transmission gap
- Apparent position anomaly

> **Analytical principle: The first task is to describe what happened in the data before attempting to explain why it happened.**

---

# 02 — Identification

The observed AIS activity must first be attributed to the correct vessel.

The analysis considers:

-IMO number
-MMSI
-Vessel name
-Call sign
-Vessel dimensions
-Vessel type
-Flag
-Historical vessel identity

Where inconsistencies are identified, they are treated as an intelligence question rather than automatically attributed to the vessel.

Potential inconsistencies may include:

- Changes in transmitted vessel identity
- Conflicting MMSI or vessel information
- Position inconsistent with vessel characteristics
- Duplicate or conflicting transmissions
- Apparent mismatch between AIS information and other available evidence

> **A transmitted identifier is not automatically treated as conclusive proof of vessel identity without consistency checks where anomalies exist.**

---

# 03 — Behavioural Analysis

The vessel's movement is analysed across time rather than as a collection of isolated positions.

The key question is:

What pattern does the vessel's movement show?

The analysis may examine:

## Route Behaviour
- Expected versus observed route
- Significant route deviations
- Repeated route patterns
- Avoidance of common navigation routes
- Movement toward or away from relevant locations
  
## Speed Behaviour
- Sudden changes in speed
- Prolonged low-speed movement
- Drifting
- Extended stationary periods
- Speed patterns inconsistent with surrounding activity
  
## Course Behaviour
- Sudden changes in heading or course
- Repeated course changes
- Circular or irregular movement patterns
- Course changes near other vessels or locations
  
## AIS Continuity
- Transmission gaps
- Unexpected loss of position reporting
- Reappearance after a reporting gap
- Changes in reporting frequency
  
## Vessel Interaction
Where available data supports it, the analysis may assess:
- Proximity to other vessels
- Extended at-sea encounters
- Parallel or coordinated movement
- Changes in movement before or after an encounter

```text  
POSITION HISTORY
        +
SPEED PATTERN
        +
COURSE PATTERN
        +
AIS CONTINUITY
        ↓
BEHAVIOURAL PATTERN
```
> **Behaviour is assessed across time. A single position rarely explains a vessel's operational activity.**

---

# 04 — Anomaly & Interference Assessment

An unusual AIS pattern requires explanation.

The analysis distinguishes between possible categories including:

- Normal operational behaviour
- Data-quality limitations
- Reception or coverage gaps
- AIS transmission interruption
- GNSS interference
- Apparent position anomaly
- Potential identity inconsistency
- Behaviour requiring further investigation

## AIS & GNSS Interference

The analysis distinguishes between:

## AIS-related anomalies
Potential inconsistencies in transmitted AIS information.

## GNSS interference
Disruption or manipulation affecting the positioning information used by navigation systems.

## Reception limitations
Gaps or inconsistencies resulting from the availability or quality of received AIS data.

> **Analytical rule: An observed anomaly is not automatically attributed to intentional manipulation without supporting evidence.**

---

# 05 — Assessment

Observed behaviour is assessed in its wider maritime context.

The analysis may correlate AIS observations with:

- Vessel characteristics
- Port activity
- Satellite imagery
- Ownership and management information
- Relevant maritime events
- Route context
- Other independently available sources
  
```text  
AIS OBSERVATION
       +
VESSEL IDENTITY
       +
MOVEMENT CONTEXT
       +
EXTERNAL INFORMATION
       ↓
INTELLIGENCE ASSESSMENT
```
The assessment separates:

| Stage | Analytical Question |
|---|---|
| **Observation** | What does the AIS data directly show? |
| **Identification** | Can the activity be reliably attributed to the vessel? |
| **Behaviour** | What movement pattern is observed? |
| **Anomaly** | What requires explanation or further investigation? |
| **Correlation** | What independent information supports or challenges the observation? |
| **Assessment** | What conclusion is justified? |

Possible analytical outcomes may include:

- Behaviour consistent with available operational context
- Anomaly identified but explanation unresolved
- Multiple signals requiring further investigation
- Behaviour supported by converging evidence

> **The assessment communicates what the evidence supports, including what remains unexplained.**

---

# 06 — Confidence

Confidence reflects the strength of the evidence supporting both:

1. The observed AIS pattern
2. The resulting behavioural assessment


| Level | Intelligence Assessment |
|---|---|
| **HIGH** | The observed behaviour is supported by consistent AIS records and corroborated by independent evidence, with limited unresolved ambiguity. |
| **MEDIUM** | A behavioural pattern is identifiable, but limitations remain in data continuity, attribution, context or independent corroboration. |
| **LOW** | Available data is incomplete, conflicting or insufficient to support a reliable behavioural assessment. |


## Confidence Factors

Confidence is determined through assessment of:

- AIS data continuity
- Position-report consistency
- Identity attribution
- Temporal coverage
- Quality and availability of received data
- Vessel operational context
- Geographic context
- Availability of independent corroboration
- Consistency between multiple data sources
- Presence of interference or data-quality concerns
- Unresolved contradictions

> **Confidence reflects the strength of the available evidence — not the certainty of the analyst.**

---

# 07 — Intelligence Assessment

Each vessel investigation is structured as:

```text
VESSEL
   ↓
AIS OBSERVATION
   ↓
IDENTITY CHECK
   ↓
MOVEMENT RECONSTRUCTION
   ↓
BEHAVIOURAL ANALYSIS
   ↓
ANOMALY ASSESSMENT
   ↓
CORROBORATION
   ↓
INTELLIGENCE ASSESSMENT
   ↓
CONFIDENCE
```

The final assessment distinguishes between:

**Observed behaviour**
What is directly shown by the available AIS record.

**Anomaly**
A pattern or event requiring explanation.

**Context**
Relevant operational or geographic circumstances.

**Correlation**
Independent information that supports or challenges the observation.

**Inference**
What the available evidence may reasonably suggest.

**Confidence**
The strength of the evidence supporting the final assessment.

> **An unusual AIS pattern is not treated as evidence of wrongdoing until the available data has been examined, contextualised and corroborated.**
